\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} compares \textsc{Trallie} with the systems reported by
\citet{arora2023evaporate} on the EVAPORATE benchmark, which comprises FDA 510(k)
documents and the movie and university verticals of SWDE \citep{hao2011swde}.
We report ClosedIE, where the attributes to extract are given, and OpenIE, where the
system must also discover them. Following \citet{arora2023evaporate}, ClosedIE is
scored with token-level Text F1 \citep{rajpurkar2016squad} and OpenIE with Pair F1.
We run \textsc{Trallie} with gpt-oss-120b \citep{openai2025gptoss} and
Qwen3.8-27B \citep{qwen38}.

\paragraph{ClosedIE.}
\textsc{Trallie} achieves the highest ClosedIE scores on all three settings.
On FDA 510(k) it reaches 95.9 Text F1 with gpt-oss-120b and 97.2 with Qwen3.8-27B,
compared with 80.1 for EVAPORATE-Code+ and 78.0 for EVAPORATE-Direct
\citep{arora2023evaporate}.
On SWDE, with gpt-oss-120b, it scores 93.2 on movie and 89.9 on university websites,
8.8 and 16.2 points above the strongest prior system in each vertical.
The gains are consistent across websites: per-website ClosedIE F1 ranges from 80.6 to
99.2 (Appendix~\ref{app:swde-per-site}, Table~\ref{tab:swde-per-site}).
\textsc{Trallie} extracts all attributes of a document in a single LLM call, whereas
EVAPORATE-Direct prompts once per attribute.

\paragraph{OpenIE.}
On FDA 510(k), \textsc{Trallie} outperforms EVAPORATE-Code+ (62.8 Pair F1),
reaching 68.6 with gpt-oss-120b and 80.2 with Qwen3.8-27B.
On SWDE the picture is mixed. \textsc{Trallie} improves on EVAPORATE-Direct in both
verticals (49.8 vs.\ 45.2 on movie; 56.3 vs.\ 53.8 on university) and on
DOM-LM \citep{deng2022domlm} for university websites, but remains below
EVAPORATE-Code+ (56.8 and 59.0) and, on movie websites, below DOM-LM (54.1).
Per-website OpenIE F1 varies widely, from 27.7 (MatchCollege) to 80.0 (Embark).

\paragraph{Where OpenIE errors come from.}
Pair F1 counts a prediction as correct only if both the value and the attribute name
match the gold annotation. Most of \textsc{Trallie}'s OpenIE errors on SWDE are naming
errors rather than extraction errors: the discovered schema paraphrases a website's
labels (for example, \emph{director} where the gold attribute is \emph{Directed by}),
so correctly extracted values are scored as wrong.
To separate naming from extraction, we map each gold attribute to the predicted
attribute whose values agree with it best and score the result with Text F1.
Under this mapping, \textsc{Trallie} scores 78.0 on movie and 66.5 on university
websites. This is a diagnostic only and is not comparable to the published Pair F1 numbers.
A second source of error is schema size: the discovered schema has 20 attributes,
so on websites with more gold attributes (e.g.\ 278 on Hollywood, 66 on MatchCollege)
the remainder cannot be matched.
\citet{arora2023evaporate} also matched attribute names against a list of alternative
valid names for SWDE that has not been released; our strict matching therefore likely
understates \textsc{Trallie}'s OpenIE scores relative to the published ones.

\paragraph{Effect of prompt design.}
Table~\ref{tab:prompt-ablation} shows the effect of \textsc{Trallie}'s revised prompts
on FDA 510(k), with the LLMs and documents held fixed.
The changes matter most for OpenIE, where Pair F1 rises from 25.3 to 68.6
(gpt-oss-120b) and from 22.8 to 80.2 (Qwen3.8-27B).
The main driver is schema discovery: instructing the model to reuse the documents' own
field labels at leaf level raises the number of gold attribute names recovered in the
discovered schema from 6 to 13 of 16 (gpt-oss-120b) and from 5 to 14 of 16
(Qwen3.8-27B).
ClosedIE, where attribute names are given, changes less (91.5 to 95.9 and 97.0 to
97.2), mostly because extraction now copies values verbatim and returns null for
missing fields.
Because these prompts were developed on FDA 510(k), the FDA numbers may be optimistic;
SWDE was held out during development, and the ClosedIE gains there suggest they
transfer.
